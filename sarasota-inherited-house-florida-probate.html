Facing an inherited property situation in Sarasota County? Navigating out-of-state ownership, heavy maintenance, and local court timelines can feel overwhelming when you are grieving. When someone passes away owning real estate in their own name in Venice, North Port, Osprey, or Sarasota, that property is essentially stuck. You cannot list it on the MLS, sign a deed over to a buyer, or legally transfer ownership until a judge signs off on the estate. This is the reality of Florida probate law.

Understanding Florida Probate Basics

In Florida, probate is a court-supervised process used to identify and gather the assets of a deceased person, pay off their debts, and distribute the remaining assets to their beneficiaries. Because real estate is a highly regulated asset, the title to a home cannot change hands automatically just because you are named in a will.

All probate cases involving property in this region are handled by the 12th Judicial Circuit Court of Florida. Depending on where the deceased lived, your filings will go through the courthouse in downtown Sarasota or the South County courthouse in Venice.

Many families mistakenly assume that if their parent had a will, probate is unnecessary. A will is simply a set of instructions to the judge; it does not bypass the court process. To actually transfer the property, the will must be admitted to the local court, and the estate must go through either Summary or Formal Administration.

Florida Formal vs. Summary Administration

When an individual passes away owning property in Florida, the estate is governed by the rules found in Florida Statute Chapter 733. Your attorney will review the assets and debts to determine which of the two primary probate tracks your family must take.

Summary Administration

This is a much faster, abbreviated probate process. According to state law, an estate qualifies for Summary Administration under two specific conditions: either the total value of the estate subject to probate is $75,000 or less, or the decedent passed away more than two years ago.

It is important to note that if the Sarasota home was the deceased person's primary residence, it might qualify as a "Protected Homestead." If the court agrees, the value of that home does not count toward the $75,000 limit. If your situation qualifies for Summary Administration, the court can issue an order distributing the property relatively quickly—often within a few months—allowing you to sell the house much faster.

Formal Administration

If the estate exceeds the $75,000 threshold and the death occurred within the last two years, you must file for Formal Administration. This is the traditional, longer probate process. All local filings and public records for this process go through the Sarasota County Clerk and Comptroller.

Formal administration involves a judge officially appointing a Personal Representative, notifying all known creditors, publishing a notice to creditors in a local newspaper, and waiting a mandatory 90-day creditor period. During this time, the home's proceeds cannot be fully distributed to the heirs until all medical bills, credit cards, and outstanding debts are paid off.

The Heavy Duties of a Personal Representative

During Formal Administration, the court will issue "Letters of Administration." This document grants a specific person—the Personal Representative (often called an executor in other states)—the legal authority to act on behalf of the estate. According to resources from The Florida Bar, this representative has a fiduciary duty to manage the estate's assets responsibly.

When an estate includes a house, the Personal Representative's job becomes physically and financially demanding. They are legally responsible for:

  • Securing the Property: Ensuring the house is locked, the yard is maintained to avoid local code violations in Sarasota, and the property is protected against vandalism.
  • Maintaining Utilities and Taxes: Paying the ongoing electric, water, and property tax bills out of the estate's funds.
  • Managing Florida Weather Risks: An empty house in Florida is highly susceptible to humidity, mold growth, and hurricane damage. If the AC breaks while the house sits vacant during a nine-month probate process, a minor issue can turn into thousands of dollars in mold remediation.
  • Updating Insurance: Standard homeowner's insurance policies often cancel coverage or deny claims if a house is left vacant for more than 30 to 60 days. The representative must usually secure an expensive "vacant home" policy to protect the asset.

Because maintaining an aging, vacant property in the Florida heat can quickly drain an estate's funds, many Personal Representatives choose to walk you through a direct sale rather than floating the costs of a traditional MLS listing.

Selling the House During the Probate Timeline

Can you sell the house before the six-to-nine-month probate process is completely finished? Yes, but there are strict rules.

Once the Personal Representative is appointed, they can sign a real estate contract to sell the home. However, depending on the specifics of the will and the court's orders, they may need to petition the judge for approval of the sale. The judge wants to ensure the home is being sold for a fair price and that the interests of the heirs and creditors are protected.

When the house is sold, the proceeds do not go directly into the heirs' bank accounts. Instead, the title company wires the money into a restricted estate bank account. Those funds sit in the estate account to satisfy any creditor claims during the mandatory 90-day waiting period. Once all debts are cleared, the judge signs an order allowing the remaining funds to be distributed to the family.

FAQ

What is Summary Administration in Florida?

Summary Administration is a faster probate process in Florida. It is available if the total value of the estate subject to probate is $75,000 or less, or if the decedent passed away more than two years ago.

How long does Formal Administration take in Sarasota County?

Formal Administration typically takes anywhere from six to nine months. However, complex estates with multiple creditor claims can extend well beyond a year depending on the Sarasota County court docket.

Can I sell an inherited property before Florida probate is completely finished?

Yes, but the Personal Representative must have the proper authority granted by the probate court to execute the sale. The proceeds from the house sale may need to be held in a restricted estate account until all creditor claims are settled.

Do I need to clean out an inherited house before selling to a direct cash buyer?

No. When selling directly to an investment company like Cash House Buyers 4 You, you can simply take the items that matter to your family and leave the rest of the debris, old furniture, and trash behind for us to handle.

Are out-of-state heirs required to attend probate hearings in Sarasota?

In most cases, out-of-state heirs do not need to appear in court. A locally hired Florida probate attorney can attend hearings on behalf of the Personal Representative and manage the filings digitally or by mail.
